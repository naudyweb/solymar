Tour of the Paracas National Reserve

Explore the only place in Peru where the desert plunges in colored cliffs straight into the Pacific Ocean.

Adventure Details

The Paracas National Reserve is Peru's only marine-coastal protected area of its kind: over 335,000 hectares where the desert, dramatic cliffs, and the cold Humboldt Current combine into landscapes you will not find anywhere else.

On this 3.5-hour land tour you will visit the famous Red Beach, the La Catedral viewpoint, the peninsula lookouts, and the fishing cove of Lagunillas, with guided photo stops at every point.

It is the perfect companion to the Ballestas Islands tour: in a single day you see both the marine and the desert side of Paracas, with hotel pick-up and drop-off included.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the Paracas National Reserve tour cost?

Our guided tour costs S/ 50 per person and includes transport, a professional guide, and hotel pick-up in Paracas. The SERNANP entrance fee is paid separately: S/ 11 adults, S/ 5 children. Real total cost for an adult: S/ 61, with no extra charges.

What will I see on the Reserve tour?

The classic circuit includes Red Beach (famous for its garnet-colored sand), the La Catedral viewpoint, the coastal desert lookouts, and Lagunillas fishing cove, where you can have a fresh seafood lunch by the ocean.

Can you swim in the reserve beaches?

Swimming is prohibited at most stops due to strong currents and wildlife protection. However, swimming is allowed at specific spots like La Mina Beach on seasonal or private tours.

How much is the park entrance fee?

The general entrance fee is S/ 11 for adults and S/ 5 for children. If you also plan to visit the Ballestas Islands, buy the combined ticket for S/ 22, valid for 2 days.

What should I bring?

Sunscreen, a hat that straps on, sunglasses, a windbreaker, water, and some cash for the entrance fee, crafts, or lunch at Lagunillas. The weather is sunny and windy almost all year round.

Is it better to visit by guided tour or by bicycle?

Cycling is scenic but demanding: the loop is over 30 km under strong sun and wind. The guided tour takes you comfortably to every viewpoint, explains the geology and wildlife, and leaves time for photos at each stop.
